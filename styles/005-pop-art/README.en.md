## Overview

Halftone dots replace gradients, and bold black outlines frame every color. An 18-second journey from collage, soup cans, comic dots, silkscreen flowers, and a soft burger to today’s emoji and comic filters.

Series: **Design styles**. Period shown in this episode: **1956—1970**.

## Five practical rules

| Rule | How to apply it |
|---|---|
| **Halftone dots** | Represent tone with dot size and consistent spacing rather than blurred gradients. |
| **Black outlines** | Enclose color areas with consistent bold ink lines and hard offset shadows. |
| **Primary colors** | Use flat red, yellow, and blue with black and white, plus selective pink or cyan accents. |
| **Repetition** | Repeat the same image in a grid, changing its colors rather than redrawing each copy. |
| **Comic lettering** | Use short, loud words in bursts; keep readable details in quiet speech or caption boxes. |

## Common pitfalls

This episode distinguishes printed Pop Art imagery from Memphis furniture-like blocks. Dots should express tone, not merely cover every surface as decoration.
